**The question:** I measured the diversity of my sample. How much of the population's diversity
does that number reflect, and have I sequenced enough?

Diversity indices computed from a sample, such as Shannon entropy, are biased: a finite sample
misses the rare sequences, so it looks less diverse than the population it came from. BEAM
computes the population's diversity directly from its model, over every possible sequence, and
how much of it a sample of a given depth is expected to show.

## The diversity of the population

`entropy` is the Shannon entropy of the model's sequence distribution, computed over every
sequence. Its exponential, $D_1$, is the **effective number of sequences**: the number of equally
common sequences that would be as diverse. `hill_number(2.0)` is the inverse Simpson index,
$D_2$, which weighs common sequences more.

<Example src="diversity/model_diversity" />

The population behind the reference model is as diverse as about 270 million equally common
sequences by Shannon entropy, and about 630 thousand by the inverse Simpson index, which counts
the common sequences most.

## How much diversity a sample shows

`sampling_deficit_many(depths)` gives the entropy a sample of each depth is expected to **lose**
to finite sampling. The sample's expected Shannon entropy is the population's minus that
deficit. `novelty_many(depths)` gives the chance that the next cell sequenced carries a sequence
not yet in the sample.

A sample of 100,000 cells shows only 59% of the population's Shannon entropy; even ten billion
cells show 95%. The chance that the next cell is new falls slowly as well: after a million cells,
the next cell still has an 82.5% chance of carrying a sequence not yet seen. The rare
sequences, each individually improbable, make up the missing part.

Use this to:

- **correct a measured diversity** for depth: the expected sample entropy at your depth is the
  baseline to compare a measured entropy against;
- **compare samples of different depths** fairly: compare each with its own expected value;
- **decide whether to sequence more**: the chance of a new sequence says how much more sequencing
  would still reveal.

The [depth slider](/BEAM/guides/sequencing-depth/) shows both quantities at any depth up to
$10^{11}$ cells.

## Comparing diversity between repertoires

To compare the diversity of repertoires sequenced to different depths, bring them to a common
depth first. `depth_matched_hill_profiles` resamples each repertoire to the smallest depth in the
set, builds a model from each resample and returns its Hill numbers; see
[Diversity at equal depth](/BEAM/reference/diversity/).

<Aside type="caution" title="Pitfalls">
- **A sample here is independent draws from the population.** The deficit is computed for a
  Poisson sample of the given depth from the model. Clonal expansion makes a real sample less
  diverse than this.
- **Use your population's model.** The numbers above describe the reference population. For a
  particular tissue or cell type, build a model of that group and ask it.
- **Entropy depends on the weighting.** A model weighted by abundance includes clonal expansion
  in its diversity; a model of unique sequences does not. See
  [Abundance or unique sequences?](/BEAM/explanations/abundance/)
</Aside>
