**The question:** in a cohort of people, how many would I expect to carry this sequence in their
sampled repertoire?

A sequence is **public** when it appears in many people. How many depends on two things: how
common the sequence is in the population, which is its probability under the model, and how
deeply each person was sampled. `PublicnessModel` combines them exactly.

## Expected carriers

Describe the cohort by the depth of each person's sample, then ask about a sequence's
probability.

`moments` returns the mean and variance of the number of carriers. The most common of the three
sequences is expected in about 81 of the 100 people; the rarest in almost nobody.

## The most public sequences of a repertoire

`moments` takes many probabilities at once, so a whole repertoire can be ranked by how public
its sequences are expected to be:

<Example src="publicness/rank" />

At this depth the most common sequences are expected in all 100 people, while 71% of this
sample's sequences are expected in fewer than one person: most of any repertoire is private. The
sequences at the top are the ones a cohort study would see again and again.

<Aside type="caution" title="Pitfalls">
- **This is the expectation from probability alone.** Sharing in real cohorts also comes from
  shared HLA types and shared exposures, such as a common virus. A sequence carried by many more
  people than expected is exactly what such an effect looks like, which is what makes the
  expectation useful.
- **Depth is the number of cells sampled per person**, not the number of distinct sequences
  observed.
- **The population matters.** Under the reference model, probabilities describe a broad
  aggregate of published repertoires. A cohort that differs from it (another species, another
  chain, a sorted subset) needs a model of its own population.
</Aside>
