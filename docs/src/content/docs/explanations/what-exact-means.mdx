"Exact" on this site means one of three things, and BEAM always tells you which.

## Exact

Probabilities of sequences, the normaliser, length probabilities, sampling in proportion to
probability, and sums over all sequences that a sweep through the model's graph can compute
directly. These are exact up to ordinary floating-point rounding, about one part in $10^{15}$.
The total probability of all sequences is one to that precision; see
[How BEAM works](/BEAM/explanations/how-beam-works/#why-the-normaliser-can-be-exact).

## Exact with a guaranteed bound

Quantities such as the expected number of distinct sequences at a depth, overlaps between
samples and sequences private to one person are integrals that BEAM evaluates numerically. Each
result comes as a **bounded value**: the number, together with a rigorous upper bound on its
relative error. The bound is not an estimate of the error; the true value is guaranteed to lie
within it.

<Example src="exactness/bounded" />

A bounded value cannot be turned into a plain number by accident. Read `.value` once you have
looked at `.bound`. Typical bounds are below one part in a trillion; a value that could not be
brought within the requested tolerance is returned with the bound it has, never silently.

## Refused

Some quantities have no exact form under the model. BEAM refuses them rather than returning
an approximation that would be easy to mistake for an exact number, and offers what *can* be
said exactly instead. For example, the diversity of a group of repertoires is exact at Hill
order 2 but has no exact form at order 1 (Shannon diversity). There, BEAM gives exact lower and
upper bounds:

<Aside type="tip" title="Why refuse?">
A number without its caveat gets copied into a table, and the caveat stays behind in the
prose. Refusing costs one line of code; the alternative costs a wrong number in a paper.
</Aside>

## What exactness does not cover

Exactness is about computing what the **model** says. Whether the model describes your
repertoire well is a different question: it depends on the data the model was built from and
on the sampling assumptions (independent draws, no clonal expansion). The guides state, for
each calculation, what it assumes.
